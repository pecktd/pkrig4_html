pkrig4.components.ik_solvers

components.ik_solvers

How a Limb's IK chain reaches its IK end.

A limb builds its IK joints and its end (wrist, foot roll), then hands the solver the transform the IK end should follow. The solver adds handles (and any helper chains or controls) to the limb and reports what the limb wires up after it: which handles the pole vector drives and which chain stretches.

RpSolverOne rotate-plane handle from the root to the IK end (arms, legs)
FlexSolverA driver chain plus a flex control that swings the last joint (digitigrade legs)

Example:

class SpiderLeg(Limb):
    parts = ("coxa", "femur", "tibia", "tarsus", "tip")
    bones = 4
    bone_labels = ("coxa", "femur", "tibia", "tarsus")

    def __init__(self, guides, **kwargs):
        super().__init__(guides, ik_solver=SpiderSolver(), **kwargs)

class IkSolver()

Base class: makes limb.ik_jnts[:limb.bones + 1] reach a target.

build fills in:

  • pole_handles: rotate-plane handles the limb's pole control drives.
  • stretch_jnts: the chain whose bone lengths the limb's stretch scales; stretch_jnts[1:limb.bones + 1] are the bones' end joints.
  • ctrls: extra controls, for the control set.

class FlexSolver()

Bases: IkSolver

A digitigrade solve, after pkrig3's ZLegRig: the last bone swings around the IK end.

A hidden driver chain solves every bone onto the target with one rotate-plane handle; it carries the stretch, and the IK joints copy its bone lengths. flex_ctrl sits at the IK end, turned like the last bone, and follows the driver's last bone. The IK chain is then solved in two parts: a rotate-plane handle up to the last bend joint (the hock), placed the last bone's length back along flex_ctrl, and a single-chain handle for the last bone. Rotating flex_ctrl swings the hock around the end.

Roles: flex_ctrl, driver_jnt (indexed), driver_ik_handle, flex_ik_handle; ik_handle is the upper handle.

Raises
ValueError
The limb has fewer than three bones.
